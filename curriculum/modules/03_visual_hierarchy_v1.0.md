# Module 3 — Visual Hierarchy

Version: 1.0.0  
Curriculum source: `design_curriculum.md` v1.0, Level 3 — Hierarchy and attention.

## Learning goal

Represent primary, secondary, tertiary, utility, and decorative priority; rank intended priority from a supplied brief separately from apparent visual attention predicted from the image.

## Evidence

Consider relative size, contrast, color, isolation, position, sharpness, orientation, subject salience, grouping, reading order, and competing focal points. A large element is not automatically the most important. The call to action or date may be primary when the communication task requires it.

## Assessment

Compare reviewed orderings with Kendall or Spearman rank correlation and pairwise agreement. Explain mismatches between intended and apparent hierarchy with references to elements and evidence. Do not claim actual audience attention without a viewer study.
